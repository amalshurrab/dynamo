## Hook patterns that worked (Arabic, B2B)
- **The customer's question:** «عميلك سأل ChatGPT قبل ما يدخل متجرك.»
- **The missing slot:** «وين اسمك في جواب ChatGPT؟»
- **The counted proof:** «957 مقالًا… كل واحد ذُكر مرة على الأقل.»
- **The rule in one line:** «مقال واحد. ثلاث نتائج: Google، ChatGPT، والجواب المباشر.»
- **The uncomfortable fact:** «الإعلان يوقف. المقال يضل.»
- **The date question:** «آخر مقال في مدونتك متى؟»
Avoid: rhetorical "did you know", generic "in today's world", metaphors, "ليس… بل".

## Evidence rules
- Name the source and the claim it supports; one source per claim; link in first comment if needed.
- Analyst predictions are "توقّع"; studies are "دراسة <institution>"; the venture's own numbers carry a date.
- Allowed today for Mathkor: Gartner 25% search-volume drop by 2026; Princeton GEO study ≈40% visibility gain from
  statistics, quotations and citations. Forbidden: admin-panel user/site counts as demand.
- An illustrative number on an image is flagged to the reviewer and never claimed as a client's result.

## Pillar structure for a launch month (B2B SaaS)
Week 1 — reveal · problem · how it works · proof · education · offer (one each).
Weeks 2–4 — three pillars rotating: **the customer's new behaviour** (AI answers), **how the product works** (anatomy,
steps, reports), **the buyer's objections** (translation, agencies, time to results), with one carousel and one poll
per week for reach, and an offer post at each week's end.
Cadence that held: one post + one story daily (story 30 min after), LinkedIn same day; carousels 5–7 slides.
