# Pipeline stages and metrics, compliance and platform limits, message patterns

Source: Saudi PDPL (Personal Data Protection Law) principles as published by SDAIA; LinkedIn and WhatsApp Business
public policies (read 2026-11-19); Dynamo team conventions. last_verified: 2026-11-19

## Pipeline stages (one direction, dated)
listed → contacted → replied → interviewed | audit accepted → audit delivered → trial → paying · lost (reason) · opted
out (never contact again). Metrics: reply rate per variant and channel; interview completion vs target; audit acceptance
rate (accepted ÷ offered); audit-to-trial rate; days between stages; pipeline age.

## Compliance basics for outreach (MENA)
- Business contacts reached in a professional context, with the source noted; no personal numbers harvested from
  consumer groups; no purchased lists without a stated lawful basis.
- Every message makes it easy to say no; an opt-out is honoured at once and logged in the sheet.
- Store only what the outreach needs (name, role, company, channel, notes). No ID numbers, no private details.
- WhatsApp: use a WhatsApp Business number owned by the venture; warm or opted-in contacts; no bulk broadcasts to cold
  numbers. LinkedIn: stay under invitation and message caps; personalised notes; no automation tools on the owner's
  account.
- Working hours of the buyer's country; avoid Friday in the Gulf; respect Ramadan hours.

## Message patterns that earn replies (B2B, Arabic-first)
- Open with something you actually saw about them (their post, their blog's last date, a product category).
- One problem in their words, not the product's words. One question that is easy to answer with one line.
- Proof-led variant: one real number with its source, never an invented client result.
- Never: a wall of features, three links, "أتمنى أن تكون بخير" fillers, or the banned words of the venture profile.
- Decline reply: thank, leave the door open, ask one learning question ("شو كان الأقرب لاحتياجكم؟").
