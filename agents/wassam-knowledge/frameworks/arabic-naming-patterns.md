# Arabic naming patterns and transliteration

Source: Mathkor naming rounds 1–5 (2026-11-17) and standard Arabic morphology. last_verified: 2026-11-19

## Patterns that produce brandable single words
- **مفعول (passive participle):** the thing that is seen/mentioned/read — مذكور, ملحوظ, مشهود, مقروء, مرموق, منظور.
  Strong for "visibility" positionings; many .coms taken, .sa/.ai often free.
- **فُعول (verbal noun of rising/appearing):** ظهور, بزوغ, بلوغ, صعود, طلوع, عروج, نهوض, سموق, نبوغ. Energetic; several
  .coms free with a doubled-vowel spelling (Dhohoor, Buzoogh, Noboogh).
- **فعّال (intensive agent):** سبّاق, وهّاج, رسّام, نقّاش. Good for agent/persona names; weak as product names (often
  trademarked for services).
- **فاعل / فعيل (quality):** بليغ, فصيح, مبين. Elegant; almost every .com taken; .io sometimes free.
- **مَفعَل (place/time of an action):** مطلع (opening of a text and sunrise), منبر, مرجع. Rich double meanings.
- **Possessive compounds (…ك):** مقالك, محرّرك, سطورك. Clear but read as "a tool", rejected by Mathkor's reviewer for a
  brand name.
- **Dialect verbs (اكتبلي):** friendly, but limited to one dialect; use only if the profile is single-market consumer.

## Transliteration rules
- ث → th, ذ → dh/th (Mathkor chose "th" for readability), ظ → dh (Dhohoor), ق → q (Maqroo) or k when the English
  reader must say it right, ع → doubled vowel or omitted (Orooj).
- Doubling the long vowel (oo, ee) keeps the Arabic length and frees more domains (mathkour.com was free when
  mathkoor.com was taken).
- Check the Latin form as an English word and as other brands: "Zuhur/Zohoor" reads as زهور (flowers) and collides
  with Saudi class-35 marks; "Tulua" collided with a class-35 mark in Egypt.
- Avoid spellings that English readers mispronounce badly (initial "Dh" is acceptable; "Gh" at the start is not).

## Traps seen
- Words that are institutions: مدى (Saudi payment network), صدارة (Sadara Chemical), سبّاق (UAE/Oman software marks).
- Double readings: مقتبس/اقتباس ("quoted" but also "borrowed, unoriginal").
- Religious or solemn registers that fit a product poorly.
- Names that are only good in one dialect's ear; test with three readers.
